Model Lab · Depth Anything V2 Small

How a network guesses depth from one photo

Scroll to follow a real NYU Depth V2 test image through the model VEO audits, from pixels to a 3D room. Every picture below comes from this scene's exported data.

640 × 480 pixels921,600 colour values
scroll ↓

Step 1 · Prepare

Resize to a multiple of 14

The image is resized so its short side is 518 pixels and both sides divide by 14, giving 518 × 686. Each colour is scaled to 0–1 and normalised with the ImageNet mean and standard deviation.

518 ÷ 14 = 37 rows686 ÷ 14 = 49 columns

Step 2 · Patchify

Cut it into 1,813 patches

A 37 × 49 grid of 14 × 14 pixel squares. Each patch is flattened into 14 × 14 × 3 = 588 numbers. From here on, the model never sees a pixel on its own again.

37 × 49 = 1,813 patches588 numbers each

Step 3 · Embed

Patches become tokens

A learned linear layer maps each patch's 588 numbers to a 384-dimensional token, and a position embedding records where it came from. A class token joins the front of the sequence, making 1,814.

588 → 384 dims+1 class token

Step 4 · Attend

Twelve blocks of self-attention

The DINOv2 ViT-Small encoder runs twelve transformer blocks. In each, six attention heads let every token weigh every other token, so a plain patch of wall can borrow depth cues from the floor edge across the room. The colours on the grid are this scene's real activation maps at blocks 3, 6, 9 and 12.

12 blocks6 heads1,814² ≈ 3.3M pairs per head

Step 5 · Tap

Four snapshots of one network

The DPT decoder takes the tokens after blocks 3, 6, 9 and 12, reshapes each back into a 37 × 49 map, and resamples them to four resolutions. Early blocks keep edges; late blocks carry scene layout.

Step 6 · Fuse

Fuse, upsample, predict

Fusion blocks merge the four maps from coarse to fine at 64 channels. A small convolutional head upsamples to full resolution and outputs one number per pixel, kept non-negative by a ReLU.

That number is not metres. This checkpoint predicts relative inverse depth: larger means nearer, with an unknown scale and offset.

Step 7 · Align

VEO turns it into metres

For each image, VEO fits a scale and a shift in disparity space against the sensor, then inverts: depth = 1 / (s · P + t), capped at 10 m. Now each patch can stand in 3D.

Step 8 · Audit

Where the guess breaks

The patches slide to the depth the Kinect actually measured and glow by error. These are the places VEO turns into regions, labels and tests.
